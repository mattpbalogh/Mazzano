This penal function evolved as the territory shifted into ecclesiastical hands. When it became part of the Papal States' Domusculta Capracorum in the 8th century and subsequently fell under the strict monastic rule of the Monastery of Saints Andrew and Gregory starting in 945 AD, the Vatican and ecclesiastical authorities capitalized on the village's fortified, maze-like geography. The de-feudalization of Mazzano Romano finally accelerated following World War II. While the transition began unfolding conceptually during the late 1940s amid deep rural socio-economic crises, it culminated legally in the sweeping Italian Agrarian Reform of 1950. Spearheaded by the government's Ente Maremma agency, the state expropriated vast tracts of ancestral land from the Del Drago princes. This historic intervention formally dismantled centuries of absolute aristocratic estate management, permanently liberating the local population from feudal dependency and distributing the land directly to the resident farmers who had worked it for generations.
